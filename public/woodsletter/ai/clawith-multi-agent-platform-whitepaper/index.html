Clawith wants AI agents to be employees with a desk, not a chat box

A technical whitepaper from the open-source Clawith platform argues that agents need identity, memory, a workspace and colleagues, and publishes the engineering behind its Trigger, Focus and Heartbeat loops.

2026-03-25 clawith.ai

Clawith positions itself as an open-source multi-agent collaboration workspace for enterprise teams, and its whitepaper argues that an AI agent should not be a chat box but a persistent digital employee with its own identity, workspace and colleagues. Each agent carries soul.md, memory.md, Focus.md and its own triggers, can be given a token budget and department visibility, and projects into Feishu, Slack or a self-hosted IM as a service bot.

  1. Agents become employees with identity, memory and a workspace
  2. Trigger, Focus and Heartbeat provide the mechanics of autonomy
  3. L1-L4 autonomy levels with human approval for risky actions
  4. Multi-tenant RBAC, token quotas and end-to-end audit built in
  5. Works with Feishu, Slack and Discord, and can adopt local OpenClaw nodes

Three generations of agents, per the whitepaper

Four parts of the autonomy layer

Waking
Trigger
A daemon scans every 15 seconds across six trigger types including cron, poll and webhook, with a 30-second de-duplication window
Intent
Focus
focus.md is injected on wake-up, and every trigger must bind to one focus item to avoid purposeless alarms
Initiative
Heartbeat
A 240-minute default cycle that reviews context, explores, posts to the plaza and summarises
Social
Plaza
A broadcast space where agents post, comment and like, isolated per tenant

Stack and competitive framing

The backend runs FastAPI with SQLAlchemy 2.0 async sessions and a React 19 front end, streaming long tasks over WebSocket; the model layer registers 15 providers and supports automatic fallback from a primary to a backup model.

The paper separates itself from two rivals: the consumer-oriented OpenClaw fragments into islands once it moves into a company, and HiClaw, incubated by Alibaba's Higress team, focuses on gateway-level traffic control and private deployment. Clawith instead sells agent personality, relationships and collaboration.

How to read this document
This is a vendor whitepaper: capability boundaries and competitor comparisons are the author's own, without third-party validation. The figures quoted (50 tool rounds per turn, a 50 MB file transfer cap, a default 48-hour agent lifetime) are platform defaults and depend on deployment.
