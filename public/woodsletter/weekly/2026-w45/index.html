Top story In market

Xunwu deploys 200+ drones and 52 AI sorting lines for its navel orange harvest

Harvest of 240,000 mu of navel oranges in Xunwu, Jiangxi, began on 6 November. More than 200 drones carry about 100 kg per trip, up to 18 tonnes a day each, at about 30% less than manual haulage, while 52 sorting lines grade fruit by weight, colour and sugar using AI vision and infrared testing.

Why it matters: Haulage, grading and pre-cooling are being equipped together across a whole county, a sign that origin automation is moving from single pilots to county-wide build-outs.

Cold chain and logistics

01

Pallets wait a median 95 minutes before cooling; Inteligistics launches per-pallet timers Launch

Data from one US cooling site showed 2,294 pallets waited a median 95 minutes from receipt to cooling over two days, and one in five waited more than three hours. InteliView Harvest-to-Cool counts down every pallet and alerts staff; it opens to Famous Software users from January 2027.

02

Reefer digital twin finds mid-truck oranges warming from 7.7°C to 10.8°C Research

Empa placed 108 sensors in a 31-pallet citrus load from Greece to Switzerland and built a digital twin of airflow and heat transfer. Although the unit was set to 4 to 6°C, oranges in the middle of the truck warmed from about 7.7°C to 10.8°C, which the model can flag as shelf-life risk.

03

JD 7Fresh's two-stage pre-cooling gets Yunnan blueberries to warehouse in 48 hours In market

Blueberries are first cooled to an 8 to 10°C core temperature after harvest, then to 1 to 3°C after sizing and packing, and loaded through sealed docks into pre-cooled trucks with core temperatures tracked. The fastest run reaches the regional warehouse in 48 hours, with a Brix floor of 10.

七鲜卓越农场, Puzhehei base · JD QixianChina云南网 (Yunnan.cn) ↗
04

Philippines opens four solar-wind modular cold stores worth 45.5 million pesos In market

The four 40-foot container units in Camarines Sur hold 1 to 10°C and run on 30 solar panels of at least 700 W, a 3 kW wind turbine and at least 160 kWh of lithium storage, with diesel backup. They serve vegetable and high-value crop growers.

Postharvest and packaging

05

Vidre+ in-pack stickers keep blueberries 45 days versus about 25 In market

Stickers placed inside existing clamshells or bags slowly release an active complex that slows softening and dehydration. In supplier-reported trials blueberries held quality for 45 days against about 25 for controls, and raspberries gave up to 40% more marketable punnets. The product is commercially available.

Poland · European UnionFreshPlaza ↗
06

Onza launches a crystallised-ozone sachet claiming 5 to 7 extra days of shelf life Launch

Ozone usually lasts about 20 minutes after generation; Onza traps it in a salt and sugar crystal that releases slowly inside the pack. Company trials report five to seven extra days, and trials are under way with a global berry grower shipping about 30 days from Morocco and a North American salad producer.

Harvesting and growing

07

Korean strawberry robot Picky reports 95% field success at 14 to 16 fruit a minute Demo

Beyond Robotics showed its two-arm harvester at KIEMSTA 2026 in Daegu. Using 3D vision for position and ripeness, it reports about 95% field success and under 1% fruit loss, and also grades, carries and recharges itself; farm deployment is planned.

08

Seed-grown hybrid strawberries halve propagation time and cut labour 80% in Beijing trial Trial

The Jingwa centre produces F1 seed from male-sterile parents instead of propagating runners, blocking virus transmission at source. Trials cut seedling time by half and labour by 80%, with selected lines above 13 Brix and 20 grams a fruit; multi-site demonstrations are planned.
