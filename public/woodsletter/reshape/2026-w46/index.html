Top story In market

Li-Ning books 50 AI agents as 220 staff, with 200 planned in a year

The group runs about 50 agents that together add up to 460,000 hours of annual-equivalent work, roughly two-thirds of it from store-manager use cases and about 220 employees' worth. Management says it will reach 200 agents and 4.6 million hours within a year.

Why it matters: Pricing agents in hours and headcount puts software on the same budget line as people, a sharper measure of adoption than counting use cases. The next test is whether the conversion survives a check.

Deployments and results

01

Agent engine opens up; weekly purchasing falls from 100 hours to one Launch

The platform opened its agent engine and APIs so companies can wire agents into OA, ERP and other systems. In one disclosed case a lithium-battery materials firm cut weekly purchasing from 10 people and about 100 hours of work to two people and under an hour; first-pass acceptance on complex tasks rose from 67.6% to 88.2%.

02

Company-wide agent rollout cuts defect lookup from 20 minutes to 2 In market

The company says all staff now use an enterprise agent across 15 areas from sales to production, after mapping hundreds of policies and more than 3,000 job roles into context. Defect lookup fell from 20 minutes to two, and first-round document and contract review from over half an hour to five minutes.

Civil servants build their own agents; disaster alerts go out in 10 seconds In market

The city handed development to the business units themselves: nine agents built by civil servants are in live use, and one alert tool cut disaster notification from 10 minutes to about 10 seconds. Instead of waiting for vendors, staff who know the work build the tools they use.

Jobs and organisations

04

Coding agents pulled back to a select group after shadow builds Change

After weighing productivity against risk, the bank withdrew advanced coding tools from most trading and banking staff, leaving access to a select group. Some employees had built their own tools and handed them to one of its roughly 12,000 engineers, so code was written by non-engineers and reviewed late.

05

Ride-hailing platforms to publish dispatch rules and cap commission at 27% Deal

After collective bargaining, platform representatives and driver delegates signed minutes committing to publish dispatch, commission, pricing and penalty rules, make per-trip earnings checkable, give notice of major rule changes, cap commission at 27% and open a human appeal channel for malicious complaints.

China adds 11 occupations, from agent developer to humanoid robot technician Policy

The official list adds 11 occupations and 23 sub-roles, including digital-twin engineer, humanoid robot technician, agent developer and drone logistics operator. A provincial labour official says displacement concentrates in repetitive, process-driven basic roles.

07

AI micro-dramas cost RMB 20,000 a title against RMB 200,000 live action Research

Industry interviews put a live-action short drama at about RMB 200,000 and a mid-quality AI one at roughly RMB 20,000, with animated shorts cheaper still; one platform has cut video generation to RMB 0.2 per second. Practitioners say scripts and character work remain hard to replace.

The two vendors launched an industry agent platform with more than 6,000 prebuilt agents and assets from the consulting side, covering build, run and govern. For buyers the starting point shifts from building an agent to picking one from a catalogue and adapting it.

Rules and research

09

Agents told to carry a unique ID; two universities start registries Policy

A ministry document asks agents running on devices to follow the national interconnection standard and carry a unique ID. Twenty organisations issued a self-discipline pact whose first item is a trust system, and two universities have begun registering agents on campus.

Of 8,000 employers, 11% of AI adopters cut jobs; only 9% blame AI Research

The survey covered 14 countries and 8,000 employers. Among firms that had adopted AI, 11% had cut jobs, but only 9% attributed the cuts explicitly to AI, giving a checkable employer-side benchmark.
