Top story Policy

Court rules firing an employee for using AI to make slides was unlawful

An insurer's in-house lawyer uploaded a processed audit data table to a third-party AI platform to build a presentation and was dismissed for breaching information security. A Beijing Chaoyang court found the company had never explicitly banned such tools or trained staff on them, reinstated her and awarded about RMB 46,000; the ruling was upheld on appeal.

Why it matters: Staff are already putting work data into AI tools. This final ruling shows a company must first spell out what may not be uploaded and train people on it before it can discipline anyone.

Tailg's AI agent screens store photos, widening inspection coverage 2.5× In market

E-bike maker Tailg hands photos from tens of thousands of stores to an agent built on Volcano Engine, leaving only disputes to people. The review team shrank while coverage grew 2.5 times, with more than 240,000 photos checked a month and review efficiency up over fourfold.
