Top story In market

Changan plant cuts BOM resolution from 15 minutes to half a second

The plant builds to order, running one process per vehicle across models and powertrains. Resolving a single vehicle's bill of materials fell from 15 minutes to 0.5 seconds, and on-time delivery reached 100%. Process engineers moved from configuring each model and costing each order to writing rules and handling exceptions.

Why it matters: Once BOM resolution and scheduling run in seconds, the economics of mixed-model lines change: small batches and many variants can be built to order. That says more about where manufacturing is being altered than another robot on the line.

Deployments and results

Shandong Energy runs 350 AI use cases across 200 sites In market

Working with Huawei since 2021, the group has built models for mining, chemicals and power and now runs more than 350 use cases at over 200 units inside and outside the group. One mine reports more than RMB 4 million a year in value, 18 fewer high-risk posts and 80% faster inspection; power-price forecasts reach 88% accuracy.

Sorting robots reach 11 postal sites in Guangdong at 1,500 parcels an hour In market

Embodied sorting robots are now deployed across 11 postal nodes in eight Guangdong cities, after a pilot in Guangzhou in March 2026. Qantas to cut 850 jobs via Accenture deal, drawing "AI washing" claims Change

Qantas plans to cut 850 roles through a technology partnership with Accenture, prompting accusations of AI washing: the work moves to the supplier rather than disappearing, which makes the real degree of automation hard to verify.

06

Microsoft offers voluntary exit to about 60,000 staff Change

Microsoft has offered voluntary exit packages to about 60,000 employees as part of a structural adjustment. The form matters: it is a voluntary scheme rather than a forced layoff, leaving the choice partly with staff and avoiding the reputational and legal cost of compulsory cuts.

Two answers to agentic shopping: Walmart issues IDs, United blocks them Launch

Walmart is preparing agent profiles that let customers authorise their own AI agents to buy, with spending caps and permissions set per agent. In the same report United Airlines blocks third-party agents from its systems and from reading fares and inventory. The split is over who owns the customer relationship.

One in five US layoff announcements blamed AI: 121,401 jobs Research

Challenger data puts layoffs explicitly attributed to AI at 121,401 in January-October 2026, or 19.7% of all announced cuts. In the same week Yelp named AI while Dole's restructuring did not, showing how much the count depends on self-reporting.

Figma: 41% of designers now build in code, up from 21% a year ago Research

Figma's 2026 AI report says 41% of designers now build interfaces in code, double the 21% a year earlier. The handover between design and implementation is thinning: instead of static files rendered by engineers, close to half of designers produce working prototypes themselves.

51% of drugmakers say AI is deployed at scale, up from 15% Research

An industry report puts healthcare AI spending at USD 3.7 billion, with 51% of pharmaceutical companies saying they have deployed AI at scale, against 15% a year earlier. 84% of R&D leaders expect AI to cut at least a year from development within three to five years.
