Global mushroom market splits as India output jumps 79.4%

Demand is recovering in the Netherlands, Spain and Italy while German market share slips and Polish farms consolidate. India added 79.4% in four years.

2026-11-02 Stefan Jansen van Nieuwenhuizenfreshplaza.com

FreshPlaza's global mushroom overview shows sharply diverging regional trends after a difficult summer. Demand has recovered in the Netherlands and Spain since September and October, and Italy reported strong October sales, yet growers in all three face higher energy, transport, labour and input costs with selling prices largely unchanged. Germany keeps stable output but loses share, Polish farms are consolidating, India is expanding fast on policy support, and North America is tightening on disease and retiring growers.

Market by market

  1. Netherlands: a hot summer hit volumes; demand and prices recovered from September and Christmas enquiries have started, with fixed-price contracts to be renegotiated higher
  2. Italy: strong October demand, growth in value-added products such as mixed trays and pre-cut fresh mushrooms, and research into pre-cooked convenience lines
  3. Germany: stable production but a falling share, a higher discounted share than other vegetables and waning interest among consumers under 29
  4. Spain: demand is up but transport costs rose about 20% year on year, drought limits wild mushrooms and cultivated crops carry supply
  5. Poland: white button mushrooms make up 82% of output, farms fell from about 3,000 to under 500, and peat scarcity pushes substrate alternatives and automation
  6. India: output rose 79.4% in four years, with MIDH support for infrastructure and spawn, while post-harvest handling and processing remain weak

The shared cost squeeze

Most markets share the same structure: energy, transport, labour, straw and peat, and packaging all cost more while selling prices have not followed. Spanish growers put transport up about 20% year on year and see yields falling on warm weather and pests. Dutch growers must renegotiate fixed-price contracts at higher levels. At Rungis in late October, French mushrooms averaged EUR 3.80 per kg against EUR 2.80 for Polish product, and EUR 4.20 against EUR 3.30 in Nantes, largely unchanged since September. Industry representatives are calling for a more balanced cost and price relationship with retailers.
